# CropAndWeed dataset statistics: observations and conclusions

Generated by `scripts/dataset_stats.py` from the COCO segmentation JSONs (`data/<Variant>/coco/segmentation/{train,val,test}.json`). Full tables/figures under `results/dataset_stats/{CropOrWeed2,Fine24}/`. All counts below are instance-level unless noted; "train" numbers are used as the reference distribution.

## Scale and resolution

The dataset has 7705 images and 78288 annotated instances (both variants; `CropOrWeed2/summary.md`, `Fine24/summary.md`). Resolution is exactly uniform — every image in every split is 1920×1088 (`CropOrWeed2/tables/resolution_distribution.csv`) — so there is no resizing/aspect-ratio confound between images; any size effect below comes from the objects themselves, not from mixed source resolutions.

## Instances per image

Median 6, mean 10.24 instances/image on train (`instances_per_image.csv`), with a long right tail: std 12.7 and a max of 206 on a single train image (val max 168, test max 166). No empty images in any split. The mean well above the median confirms a small number of densely-annotated images dominate the instance count.

## Class imbalance

**CropOrWeed2**: train has 13,923 Crop vs 38,019 Weed instances, a ~1:2.7 ratio (`CropOrWeed2/tables/class_distribution.csv`), consistent across splits (test 22.6%/77.4%, val 29.5%/70.5% Crop/Weed).

**Fine24**: heavy long tail. Train counts range from 153 (Chickweed) to 12,936 (Grasses) — an ~85x spread (`Fine24/tables/class_distribution.csv`). The five rarest train classes are Chickweed (153), Potato (261), Pumpkin (388), Pea (443) and Labiate (455), together <1.4% of instances, while Grasses (24.9%) and Thistle (11.4%) alone make up over a third of all instances (`Fine24/summary.md`).

## Object size: native buckets vs. effective size after resize

Pooled over all splits, native bbox-area buckets are tiny 0.5%, small 34.4%, medium 40.8%, large 24.4% (`*/summary.md`). This looks manageable at full 1920×1088 resolution, but **degrades sharply once images are letterbox-resized for training** (`Fine24/tables/effective_size_at_imgsz.csv`, `figures/effective_size_at_imgsz.png`):

| imgsz | tiny | small | tiny+small |
|---|---|---|---|
| 640 | 52.0% | 23.6% | 75.6% |
| 1024 | 30.5% | 29.7% | 60.3% |
| 1280 | 18.9% | 33.1% | 52.0% |

(train split; test is worse at every imgsz, e.g. 60.5% tiny at 640 vs train's 52.0%). This is the core empirical motivation for the project's focus on tiling/SAHI, multi-scale training, and high-res-aware architectures: at the common YOLO default of imgsz=640, over half of all instances fall into the smallest bucket, and even at imgsz=1280 (2x the default) nearly a fifth are still tiny.

## Per-class size and the count-vs-size relationship

Train median bbox side length (`Fine24/tables/size_percentiles.csv`) ranges from 22.2 px (Labiate) and 25.1 px (Mercuries) up to 224.3 px (Potato) and 199.2 px (Pumpkin) — roughly a 10x spread between the smallest and largest classes. The numerically dominant classes (Grasses 31.5 px, Geranium 31.4 px, Mercuries 25.1 px) skew small, while several of the rarer classes (Potato, Pumpkin, Maize 115.2 px, Sunflower 93.0 px) are individually large plants (`Fine24/tables/count_vs_median_size.csv` shows the same pattern for CropOrWeed2: Weed, 2.7x more numerous than Crop, has a smaller median bbox area — 1330 px² vs 7205 px²). *Interpretation*: this is consistent with weeds/grasses being numerous, small, and often crowded, while crop plants are fewer but individually larger — but the correlation is not perfectly monotonic (e.g. Chickweed is both rare and small) so this should be read as a tendency, not a rule.

## Aspect ratio, spatial bias, border truncation, mask fill

- **Aspect ratio**: median close to 1.0 (square-ish boxes) for nearly every class (`Fine24/tables/aspect_ratio.csv`, e.g. Bean 1.00, Knotweed 1.00, Labiate 0.87), with Grasses notably more elongated (p95 AR 3.29 vs ~1.7–2.4 for most other classes) — expected for blade-shaped leaves.
- **Spatial bias**: box centres are strongly concentrated near image centre in all three splits (`figures/spatial_heatmap.png`), consistent with the camera being aimed at a fixed plot centre per session. *Implication*: models may learn a centre prior; edge regions are comparatively under-represented in every split.
- **Border truncation**: a non-trivial fraction of instances touch the image border — CropOrWeed2 train: Crop 16.8%, Weed 9.9% (`CropOrWeed2/tables/border_truncation.csv`); Fine24 weighted average per split ≈ 10.4% (test), 11.8% (train), 13.9% (val). These are partially-visible objects, relevant for both detection (partial boxes) and segmentation (partial masks).
- **Mask fill ratio** (`mask_area / bbox_area`): median ranges from 0.30 (Grasses) to 0.58 (Potato weed) across Fine24 classes (`Fine24/tables/mask_fill_ratio.csv`), i.e. typically only 30–50% of a bounding box is actually object pixels. Fallback-to-rectangle masks (boxes with no matching mask pixels) are negligible: at most 0.38% of a class's instances (Potato, train), in line with the 15 Fine24 fallback boxes reported in `data/README.md`. *Implication*: bbox-only detection wastes a large fraction of each box on background, and the derived instance masks are usable as segmentation ground truth.

## Crowding / overlap

Overlapping pairs (IoU > 0) are common relative to instance count (e.g. train: 10,278 pairs over 51,942 boxes), but *heavy* overlap is rare: only 6 pairs (train), 5 (test), 0 (val) exceed IoU 0.5 (`*/tables/crowding_summary.csv`). Median of per-image median nearest-neighbour distance is ~230–250 px across splits. *Interpretation*: dense, cluttered scenes exist but severe box overlap that would stress NMS is uncommon dataset-wide; this is a mild rather than dominant concern.

## Split representativeness

The session-grouped, Fine24-stratified split (`data/README.md` § Split) keeps distributions close but not identical to train:
- **Size-bucket shift**: Jensen–Shannon divergence of the size-bucket distribution vs. train is small but non-zero — val 0.00225, test 0.00642 (`*/tables/js_divergence_size_bucket.csv`), and test is consistently the more divergent split, matching its higher tiny-object share noted above (e.g. tiny bucket at raw scale: test 0.62% vs train 0.42%, `tables/size_bucket_per_split.csv`).
- **Low-session classes** (`Fine24/tables/low_session_classes_flagged.csv`): several rare classes are backed by very few *sessions* in val/test even when instance counts look adequate — Corn spurry (val: 4 sessions), Pea (test: 4, val: 4), Potato (test: 4, val: 2), Pumpkin (test: 2, val: 3). *Implication*: per-class AP for these classes in val/test will be estimated from only a handful of distinct plots/sessions, so it should be reported with caution (high variance, not necessarily representative of the class in general) — this is a direct consequence of the session-grouping needed to avoid near-duplicate leakage.

## Implications for the experiments

- The imgsz=640→1280 effective-size table is the main quantitative case for evaluating tiling/SAHI and higher-resolution training: even at imgsz=1280, ~52% of train instances remain tiny+small.
- Test set is measurably harder (more small objects, higher JS divergence) than train/val, so absolute metrics should be compared split-aware rather than assuming test mirrors train.
- Per-class AP on Fine24 for Corn spurry, Pea, Potato, Pumpkin and similar rare classes should be reported with explicit caveats about low session support in val/test.
- Border truncation (~10–14%) and low median mask-fill (~0.3–0.5) both argue for including instance segmentation (not just detection) in the det-vs-seg comparison, since bbox IoU alone overstates localization quality for elongated/partial objects. Caveat: instance masks are derived (bbox ∩ semantic mask), not hand-drawn, even though rectangle fallbacks are negligible (<0.4% per class).
- Heavy occlusion/overlap is not a first-order concern dataset-wide, so NMS/overlap-handling choices are likely secondary to the small-object-scale problem.
- Strong central spatial bias suggests test-time augmentation or tiling strategies should be checked for whether they preserve or disrupt this prior, since edge regions are comparatively data-poor at training time.
